A softer rule

The neck gets its own routine.

  • Gentle cleansing as needed, your tolerated moisturizer, and SPF on exposed skin.
  • Keep retinol, vitamin C, acids, and scrubs off the sensitive neck in this plan. Face tolerance doesn’t guarantee neck tolerance.
  • If skin stings or feels raw, pause new products and actives. Persistent or worsening irritation deserves a dermatologist’s advice.

Make it feel possible

No splashy sink ritual required.

Micellar water on a soft, well-saturated pad is the low-splash option. Press and sweep gently; don’t scrub. Sensibio H₂O is designed to be left on, but rinse gently if residue bothers your skin.

Prefer the shower? Use lukewarm water and your fingertips, then pat dry. For stubborn makeup or sunscreen, remove it gently first and follow with a rinse-off cleanser if needed.

Less pressure, more patience

Start Slowly

Comfort comes before the calendar. Stay in any stage longer, or keep the basics as your entire routine.

One new product at a time

Suggested introduction timeline for this version. It is not a prescribed treatment plan. Stages never advance automatically; choosing one updates Today and The Week.

01 · First 2 weeks or longer

Make the basics feel easy.

Use tolerated cleanser, moisturizer, and SPF. Introduce any new basic product separately. No vitamin C or retinol yet.

02 · Next 2 weeks or longer

A little vitamin C.

Once the basics feel comfortable, try vitamin C on Tuesday and Saturday mornings. Face only. Keep every evening simple.

03 · Next 2–4 weeks or longer

One retinol evening.

If appropriate and tolerated, add retinol on Wednesday only. Keep vitamin C at its existing frequency. No other new actives.

04 · Only when comfortable

Find your gentle rhythm.

Try Wednesday and Sunday retinol nights, with recovery nights between. Vitamin C stays Tuesday and Saturday. More is optional, never a goal.

Before the first full-face use

Give new products a small test.

The American Academy of Dermatology suggests testing new skincare on a small area, such as the inner arm, twice daily for 7–10 days; follow the product’s directions, especially for rinse-off products. For retinol, ask a dermatologist about suitability and testing if your skin is very sensitive.

A successful small-area test cannot guarantee full-face tolerance. Wash off and stop a product if you develop a reaction.

Read AAD’s testing guide (new tab)

On retinol nights

Keep it small. Keep it gentle.

Cleanse, let skin dry, and apply a light layer of moisturizer. Use a small amount of retinol according to the label on the face, avoiding eyelids, lip edges, nostril creases, and the neck; finish with moisturizer if needed.

Skip exfoliating acids, scrubs, and other retinoids that night. Don’t use retinoids during pregnancy; if trying to conceive or breastfeeding, check with your clinician before using them.

Read AAD’s retinoid guidance (new tab)
If your skin asks for a pause

Use “Keep it gentle” in Today to remove vitamin C and retinol from the schedule. Return to tolerated basics. Burning, persistent redness, itching, or peeling are reasons to stop the new active, not push through or add more products. Seek medical advice for severe, persistent, or worsening symptoms.

Protect exposed face, neck, and ears with broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher and shade or clothing. For swimming or sweating, choose a product labeled water resistant and follow its reapplication directions.
