01

Goal (The Strategic Vision)

The broad, long-term destination your organization wants to reach

Expected Characteristics

  • High-level and aspirational
  • Often qualitative, not quantitative
  • Provides the “why” — the reason you’re moving
  • Long-term oriented (typically 3-5+ years)

Development Questions

Purpose & Direction

  • ?What fundamental change do we want to create in our organization or market?
  • ?What does ultimate success look like for us in 3-5 years?
  • ?Why does this matter to our stakeholders and mission?

Clarity & Meaning

  • ?Can we describe what NOT achieving this would look like?
  • ?Is this a meaningful destination or just a values statement in disguise?
  • ?Would different teams interpret this goal the same way?

Validation Checklist

  • It’s aspirational and inspiring, not just incremental improvement
  • It provides clear direction without prescribing specific tactics
  • You can picture what failure to achieve it looks like
  • It’s qualitative — not a number to hit
  • Everyone can explain it without reading it verbatim

Common Pitfalls

  • ―Written to sound impressive rather than to guide action
  • ―So vague that any outcome could be claimed as success
  • ―Confusing a values statement (“be customer-centric”) with a goal
  • ―Approved once and never referenced again

Examples

Right

  • ✓Become the most customer-centric software company in our industry
  • ✓Transform into a data-driven decision-making organization

Avoid

  • ✗Increase revenue (this is an objective, not a goal)
  • ✗Deliver excellence (too vague, really a value)

02

Objective (The Specific Target)

A specific outcome that advances the goal

Expected Characteristics

  • Mid-term and time-bound (quarterly or annual)
  • Clearly defined and specific
  • Directly aligned with the overarching Goal
  • At the right altitude — not too high (restating goal) or too low (a task)

Development Questions

Specificity & Focus

  • ?What specific outcome will move us closer to our goal?
  • ?By when do we need to achieve this?
  • ?What will be fundamentally different when this is complete?

Altitude Check

  • ?Is this a stepping stone to the goal, or just restating the goal?
  • ?Are there multiple ways to achieve this, or is it prescribing one task?
  • ?Does this create focus without eliminating all optionality?

Alignment

  • ?How does achieving this advance our stated goal?
  • ?If we achieve this but nothing else, will we feel we made progress?

Validation Checklist

  • It’s specific enough to create focus and prioritization
  • It’s broad enough to allow multiple paths to success
  • It has a clear timeframe (by Q3, by end of year, etc.)
  • You can picture what “done” looks like
  • It directly contributes to achieving the Goal
  • It’s an outcome, not an activity or task

Common Pitfalls

  • ―Too high: just restating the goal in different words
  • ―Too low: actually a task or deliverable
  • ―Written vaguely because specific targets feel risky
  • ―No clear timeframe or deadline

Examples

Right

  • ✓Launch a customer loyalty rewards program by Q3
  • ✓Establish a real-time data pipeline for analytics by end of year

Avoid

  • ✗Improve customer experience (too high — restates goal)
  • ✗Configure the database schema (too low — it’s a task)

03

Key Result (The Success Metric)

The quantifiable outcome that proves you achieved your objective

Expected Characteristics

  • Quantifiable and measurable
  • Shows progress toward the objective
  • Often scored (reaching 70% is meaningful progress)
  • Ambitious but achievable

Development Questions

Measurement

  • ?What metrics will prove we achieved the objective?
  • ?What are we measuring from (baseline) and to (target)?
  • ?Can we actually collect this data reliably?

Ambition vs. Achievability

  • ?Are we already confident we’ll hit this, or does it require real effort?
  • ?What would reaching 70% of this target tell us?
  • ?Is this measuring ambition or just measuring activity?

Outcome Focus

  • ?Does this measure the outcome of the objective, not just output?
  • ?If we hit this number but the objective failed, would we know something’s wrong?

Validation Checklist

  • It’s a number with a clear starting point and target
  • It measures outcome (impact) not just output (activity)
  • We know how to measure it and have access to the data
  • It’s ambitious enough that 70% completion still shows real progress
  • Multiple key results together prove objective success
  • Hitting this number actually means the objective was achieved

Common Pitfalls

  • ―Sandbagging — setting targets you’re already confident you’ll hit
  • ―Measuring activity (tasks completed) instead of outcome (impact achieved)
  • ―Too many key results for one objective (more than 3-5 is noise)
  • ―Unmeasurable or requiring data you don’t have

Examples

Right

  • ✓Increase adoption from 42% to 70%
  • ✓Reduce cycle time from 28 days to 14 days
  • ✓Complete 90% of modernization roadmap

Avoid

  • ✗Launch 5 features (measures output, not outcome)
  • ✗Improve satisfaction (not quantified)

04

KPI (The Health Check)

An ongoing operational metric monitoring how efficiently your business runs

Expected Characteristics

  • Tracked continuously (weekly/monthly)
  • Monitors steady-state health
  • Flags problems early
  • Persists after objectives are achieved to prevent backsliding

Development Questions

Operational Health

  • ?What metric tells us our operations are running smoothly?
  • ?What would we watch to catch problems before they become crises?
  • ?What should we monitor even after our objective is achieved?

Cadence & Actionability

  • ?How often do we need to check this to be useful?
  • ?If this metric moves in the wrong direction, what action do we take?
  • ?Is this measuring something we can actually influence?

Focus

  • ?Do we actually make decisions based on this metric?
  • ?Would removing this from our dashboard change our behavior?

Validation Checklist

  • It monitors ongoing operations, not a one-time achievement
  • It’s tracked regularly (weekly, monthly, not annually)
  • Movement in this metric triggers specific actions
  • It’s truly a “key” metric — not just interesting data
  • It helps prevent backsliding after objectives are met
  • The team knows who owns it and who acts on it

Common Pitfalls

  • ―KPI proliferation — tracking 40 metrics until none are actionable
  • ―Never retiring old KPIs when they’re no longer relevant
  • ―Confusing KPIs (ongoing health) with Key Results (one-time achievement)
  • ―Tracking things that don’t drive decisions

Examples

Right

  • ✓Monthly customer churn rate
  • ✓Average support ticket resolution time
  • ✓Weekly system uptime percentage

Avoid

  • ✗Project completion by Q3 (that’s a milestone, not ongoing)
  • ✗Annual revenue (too infrequent to be a health check)

05

Milestone (The Progress Checkpoint)

A time-bound event showing you’re moving forward

Expected Characteristics

  • Specific and time-bound
  • Output-focused (deliverable or event)
  • Non-numerical — usually done/not done
  • Shows schedule integrity, not outcome quality

Development Questions

Deliverable & Deadline

  • ?What specific output or event marks this checkpoint?
  • ?By what date must this be completed?
  • ?What deliverable exists after this milestone that didn’t exist before?

Progress Indication

  • ?Does hitting this milestone mean we’re on track to the objective?
  • ?What dependencies unlock when this milestone is achieved?
  • ?Is this milestone a clear “done/not done” without ambiguity?

Schedule vs. Success

  • ?Could we achieve this milestone on time but still fail the objective?
  • ?What would tell us the deliverable is quality, not just on schedule?

Validation Checklist

  • It’s a clear deliverable or event, not a measurement
  • It has a specific deadline
  • It’s unambiguously done or not done
  • Achieving it indicates forward progress on the plan
  • It’s meaningful enough to be worth tracking
  • The team knows what “done” looks like

Common Pitfalls

  • ―Treating milestone completion as proof of objective success
  • ―Too many milestones — creating tracking overhead without value
  • ―Milestones that are actually Key Results (measurements, not deliverables)
  • ―False sense of progress in fast-moving environments where plans change

Examples

Right

  • ✓Finalize vendor contract by March 15th
  • ✓Complete user testing by end of Q2
  • ✓Deploy to production by June 30th

Avoid

  • ✗Improve adoption by 20% (that’s a Key Result)
  • ✗Work on the prototype (not specific or time-bound)
