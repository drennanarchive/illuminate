Does NSPM-11 or NSPM-12 remove anything from ICD 505?

No, not explicitly. NSPM-11's only rescission clause names NSM-25 by title and nothing else. NSPM-12 goes further in the opposite direction. Section 9 states the memorandum "shall not be construed to implicitly alter or supersede existing authorities or contravene existing law, Executive Orders, or Presidential Directives." Neither document names any IC Directive.

There is a catch worth the CDAO's attention. NSPM-11 Section 3(e) requires IC elements to update their internal guidance for consistency once the CNSS/OMB AI-in-NSS policy and classified annex are issued, due September 3, 2026, and to review it annually after that. ICD 505 is unchanged today, but it is on a clock. Getting ahead of that date is the point of the first item below.

What she holds directly
ICD 504 states that element CDOs must possess "authority to make decisions about developing/implementing data governance." That is affirmative authority, not a coordinating role, and it is stronger than anything found on the AI side.
Carve-outs
NSS data assets are excluded from the comprehensive data inventory requirement (44 U.S.C. 3511(a)(2)(B)). This does not exempt her other CDO duties.
Open questions
None at the level found on the AI side. The main open item concerns coordination: how ICD 504 interfaces with the newly re-established government-wide CDO Council.

The CDAO role

The Chief Data and AI Officer holds the merged CAIO and CDO portfolio at the element level, and now reports to a higher leader following the recent organizational flattening. Her mandate spans two authorities of different legal weight: coordinative influence over AI governance under ICD 505, and direct decision authority over data governance under ICD 504.

How the support team assists her

The support team exists so the CDAO can make fully informed decisions. It tracks the policy environment, develops work product, drafts materials, and prepares briefings. She reviews, decides, and directs. The team holds no decision authority of its own; every output here is prepared for her review, not issued in the team's name.

Does supporting the CIO on cyber and infrastructure AI create a conflict?

No conflict found in the text reviewed. ICD 505 directs the CAIO to coordinate with the IC CIO so the IC Information Environment supports AI implementation, so this kind of cross-functional work is built into the framework rather than an edge case it overlooked. NSPM-12 names the CIO and CISO as the reporting channel for cybersecurity requirements to CNSS. It does not restrict who may perform the underlying work.

The open question is not permission. It is governance. Any AI capability built or deployed as part of this work still falls under ICD 505's inventory and audit requirements, the same as any other AI use in the element, regardless of which office requested it.

Where the real risk sits

The CDAO both directs this work and is accountable for governing AI use under ICD 505. That is not a violation of anything written, but it places the same office in the position of doer and reviewer for its own output. The safeguard is procedural, not organizational: route this work through the same inventory, impact assessment, and reporting process any other AI use in the element goes through, rather than treating it as separate because it originated from a CIO tasking.

What is not yet confirmed

No public directive defines element-level CIO duties the way ICD 505 and ICD 504 define CAIO and CDO duties. ICD 500 covers the DNI-level, community-wide IC CIO role only. If an internal element CIO charter or delegation memo exists, it is the next document to check, and it belongs with the other pending items on the Compliance Actions tab.

Known limitations

This brief reflects unclassified public text, read directly and checked for rescission and supersession language. It does not include NSPM-11's classified annex, due September 3, which may resolve the ICD 505 question directly. It does not include any classified guidance beneath ICD 505 or ICD 504, the full text of DOD Directive 3000.09, or the underlying Executive Orders beyond how the memos cited them. Whether CNSS met its July 12 and August 11 deadlines is treated here as a textual fact, not a confirmed outcome. Most importantly, it does not yet include any internal ODNI delegation memo, CAIO or CDO Council charter, or office-specific instruction beneath the ICD level.

Treat this as the confirmed baseline to check the classified and internal layer against, not yet the final word on her operating authority.
