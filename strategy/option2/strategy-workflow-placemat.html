DO Strategy
Workflow Placemat

Question guide to validate your strategy inputs at each step

How to use it

Move through the five components in sequence. Use the development questions to shape each input, then apply the checklist, pitfalls, and examples as a quality review before moving forward.

A working guide, not a form to fill blindly.

The questions are prompts for discussion. They are intentionally static: read them, use them in a workshop, or print the page for a facilitated review.

The validation statements are optional working controls. They are the only checkable elements on the page, and selections are saved locally in the current browser.

Step 01

Goal (The Strategic Vision)

The broad, long-term destination your organization wants to reach

Expected characteristics

  • High-level and aspirational
  • Often qualitative, not quantitative
  • Provides the "why" - the reason you're moving
  • Long-term oriented (typically 3-5+ years)

A

Development questions

Use these prompts to sharpen the component before it enters a plan or review.

Purpose & Direction

  1. 01 What fundamental change do we want to create in our organization or market?
  2. 02 What does ultimate success look like for us in 3-5 years?
  3. 03 Why does this matter to our stakeholders and mission?

Clarity & Meaning

  1. 04 Can we describe what NOT achieving this would look like?
  2. 05 Is this a meaningful destination or just a values statement in disguise?
  3. 06 Would different teams interpret this goal the same way?

Common pitfalls

Watch for these failure modes

  • Written to sound impressive rather than to guide action
  • So vague that any outcome could be claimed as success
  • Confusing a values statement ("be customer-centric") with a goal
  • Approved once and never referenced again

Examples

What strong and weak versions look like

Strong

  • ✓Become the most customer-centric software company in our industry
  • ✓Transform into a data-driven decision-making organization

Needs work

  • ×Increase revenue (this is an objective, not a goal)
  • ×Deliver excellence (too vague, really a value)

Step 02

Objective (The Specific Target)

A specific outcome that advances the goal

Expected characteristics

  • Mid-term and time-bound (quarterly or annual)
  • Clearly defined and specific
  • Directly aligned with the overarching Goal
  • At the right altitude - not too high (restating goal) or too low (a task)

A

Development questions

Use these prompts to sharpen the component before it enters a plan or review.

Specificity & Focus

  1. 01 What specific outcome will move us closer to our goal?
  2. 02 By when do we need to achieve this?
  3. 03 What will be fundamentally different when this is complete?

Altitude Check

  1. 04 Is this a stepping stone to the goal, or just restating the goal?
  2. 05 Are there multiple ways to achieve this, or is it prescribing one task?
  3. 06 Does this create focus without eliminating all optionality?

Alignment

  1. 07 How does achieving this advance our stated goal?
  2. 08 If we achieve this but nothing else, will we feel we made progress?

Common pitfalls

Watch for these failure modes

  • Too high: just restating the goal in different words
  • Too low: actually a task or deliverable
  • Written vaguely because specific targets feel risky
  • No clear timeframe or deadline

Examples

What strong and weak versions look like

Strong

  • ✓Launch a customer loyalty rewards program by Q3
  • ✓Establish a real-time data pipeline for analytics by end of year

Needs work

  • ×Improve customer experience (too high - restates goal)
  • ×Configure the database schema (too low - it's a task)

Step 03

Key Result (The Success Metric)

The quantifiable outcome that proves you achieved your objective

Expected characteristics

  • Quantifiable and measurable
  • Shows progress toward the objective
  • Often scored (reaching 70% is meaningful progress)
  • Ambitious but achievable

A

Development questions

Use these prompts to sharpen the component before it enters a plan or review.

Measurement

  1. 01 What metrics will prove we achieved the objective?
  2. 02 What are we measuring from (baseline) and to (target)?
  3. 03 Can we actually collect this data reliably?

Ambition vs. Achievability

  1. 04 Are we already confident we'll hit this, or does it require real effort?
  2. 05 What would reaching 70% of this target tell us?
  3. 06 Is this measuring ambition or just measuring activity?

Outcome Focus

  1. 07 Does this measure the outcome of the objective, not just output?
  2. 08 If we hit this number but the objective failed, would we know something's wrong?

Common pitfalls

Watch for these failure modes

  • Sandbagging - setting targets you're already confident you'll hit
  • Measuring activity (tasks completed) instead of outcome (impact achieved)
  • Too many key results for one objective (more than 3-5 is noise)
  • Unmeasurable or requiring data you don't have

Examples

What strong and weak versions look like

Strong

  • ✓Increase adoption from 42% to 70%
  • ✓Reduce cycle time from 28 days to 14 days
  • ✓Complete 90% of modernization roadmap

Needs work

  • ×Launch 5 features (measures output, not outcome)
  • ×Improve satisfaction (not quantified)

Step 04

KPI (The Health Check)

An ongoing operational metric monitoring how efficiently your business runs

Expected characteristics

  • Tracked continuously (weekly/monthly)
  • Monitors steady-state health
  • Flags problems early
  • Persists after objectives are achieved to prevent backsliding

A

Development questions

Use these prompts to sharpen the component before it enters a plan or review.

Operational Health

  1. 01 What metric tells us our operations are running smoothly?
  2. 02 What would we watch to catch problems before they become crises?
  3. 03 What should we monitor even after our objective is achieved?

Cadence & Actionability

  1. 04 How often do we need to check this to be useful?
  2. 05 If this metric moves in the wrong direction, what action do we take?
  3. 06 Is this measuring something we can actually influence?

Focus

  1. 07 Do we actually make decisions based on this metric?
  2. 08 Would removing this from our dashboard change our behavior?

Common pitfalls

Watch for these failure modes

  • KPI proliferation - tracking 40 metrics until none are actionable
  • Never retiring old KPIs when they're no longer relevant
  • Confusing KPIs (ongoing health) with Key Results (one-time achievement)
  • Tracking things that don't drive decisions

Examples

What strong and weak versions look like

Strong

  • ✓Monthly customer churn rate
  • ✓Average support ticket resolution time
  • ✓Weekly system uptime percentage

Needs work

  • ×Project completion by Q3 (that's a milestone, not ongoing)
  • ×Annual revenue (too infrequent to be a health check)

Step 05

Milestone (The Progress Checkpoint)

A time-bound event showing you're moving forward

Expected characteristics

  • Specific and time-bound
  • Output-focused (deliverable or event)
  • Non-numerical - usually done/not done
  • Shows schedule integrity, not outcome quality

A

Development questions

Use these prompts to sharpen the component before it enters a plan or review.

Deliverable & Deadline

  1. 01 What specific output or event marks this checkpoint?
  2. 02 By what date must this be completed?
  3. 03 What deliverable exists after this milestone that didn't exist before?

Progress Indication

  1. 04 Does hitting this milestone mean we're on track to the objective?
  2. 05 What dependencies unlock when this milestone is achieved?
  3. 06 Is this milestone a clear "done/not done" without ambiguity?

Schedule vs. Success

  1. 07 Could we achieve this milestone on time but still fail the objective?
  2. 08 What would tell us the deliverable is quality, not just on schedule?

Common pitfalls

Watch for these failure modes

  • Treating milestone completion as proof of objective success
  • Too many milestones - creating tracking overhead without value
  • Milestones that are actually Key Results (measurements, not deliverables)
  • False sense of progress in fast-moving environments where plans change

Examples

What strong and weak versions look like

Strong

  • ✓Finalize vendor contract by March 15th
  • ✓Complete user testing by end of Q2
  • ✓Deploy to production by June 30th

Needs work

  • ×Improve adoption by 20% (that's a Key Result)
  • ×Work on the prototype (not specific or time-bound)

The discipline beneath the framework

A shared vocabulary is useful only when it improves the quality of the conversation.

Use the placemat to expose ambiguity before work begins, distinguish outcomes from activity, and make success concrete enough for teams to act with confidence.
