Companion to the Leader Operating Ecosystem

What to reach for, and when

Fourteen worksheets, canvases and reference pages sit under this. You will never need to know their filenames. What follows is organised by the thing you are actually trying to sort out — find the sentence that sounds like your week, and the instrument is underneath it.

How this gets used

Most of these are meant to be filled in with someone holding the pen while you talk. A few are just reading. The dot tells you which.

We sit down and do it together Read on your own, whenever

“This landed on my desk and I’m not sure it should have.”

The question underneath is which decisions are genuinely yours. With no layer above you, anything that isn’t yours has to move down to the team or sit unowned — there is no third direction.

State of Play
The short list of what currently needs you, refreshed each standup. Its filter rule is the working answer: something reaches you only if you decide it, you’ll be asked about it, or something changed materially.
Together · 5 min · ongoing
Team Cadence Builder — step 3
One step of a longer worksheet, but the relevant one: what the team decides alone versus what needs your sign-off, written down rather than assumed.
Together · 15 min for that step
Thin here Nothing in the set does a full inventory of what’s yours versus what isn’t. Five tools touch it; none of them go deep.

“I keep finding out about things too late.”

Usually a cadence problem rather than a people problem. What reaches you, how often, and in what shape — and what the escalation channel is when something can’t wait for the next scheduled thing.

Team Cadence Builder
Five steps, autosaves. Inventories every recurring touchpoint, then defines one key loop: what goes up, what comes back down, what counts as urgent.
Together · 45 min · do once, revisit yearly
Leadership Operating Rhythm
One page showing four tiers of engagement — operational sync, monthly touchpoint, executive prep, quarterly planning — with what each produces. Useful as a picture of what a full rhythm looks like before designing your own.
On your own · 3 min read

“Two people are circling the same work and I can’t tell who owns it.”

Ownership questions look like personnel questions and usually aren’t. The useful first move is naming the one capability actually needed, before naming a person.

Role Evaluation Framework
Three questions on one page: what’s the one thing we need, are they good at it, how do we make sure they do it. Ends on the hard one — are you willing to structure accountability around their weakness.
Together · 15 min · reusable, refill each time
Role Alignment Canvas
The longer version, when the answer needs to leave the room. Produces a printable brief: capability, fit, boundaries, accountability, decision.
Together · 40 min · produces a document

“Is a standing monthly review worth my calendar?”

A fair question to ask before anyone builds one. There is a pitch and a working version, and you can decide on the pitch alone.

Monthly Touchpoint Concept
A single slide making the case, including what you’d owe the team in return — organisational context, priority alignment, direction, decisions, external insight. Read it and say yes or no.
On your own · 2 min read
PMR Lite Pre-Read
What you’d actually receive each month if you said yes. Worth skimming so the answer is informed — note the two sections you get nowhere else: what changed around us, and strategic signals.
On your own · 4 min skim
Package Walkthrough
Only if you want the reasoning. Explains why the rhythm map, the pitch and the pre-read are three zoom levels rather than three drafts, and the order they’re meant to be used in.
On your own · optional

“My scope just grew and I haven’t reset anything.”

The most under-used tool in the set, and the one written for exactly this situation. Worth doing deliberately rather than absorbing the new load and hoping the old rhythm still fits.

Leadership Operating Rhythm Workshop
Six steps, built for a leader whose responsibilities just expanded. Opens with three questions worth sitting with even if you go no further: what responsibilities increased, what will drain your time, what cannot fall through the cracks.
Together · 60 min · do once after a change

“I want to know what the team is doing without having to ask.”

Two different rhythms answer this. One is the running picture; the other is the monthly step back. They are not substitutes — the first tells you what’s live, the second tells you what moved and what it means.

State of Play
Three to five items needing your call, plus workstream status. Deliberately short — if it grows past five, that’s a signal in itself.
Together · 5 min · refreshed each standup
PMR Lite Pre-Read
The monthly version, if adopted. Ahead of the discussion rather than during it, so the meeting can start at the decisions.
On your own · 10 min · monthly

“What is this team actually for?”

The foundational exercise, and the longest. Worth doing when the answer genuinely isn’t settled — not as a documentation task.

Together · several sittings · the deep one
Operating Model Overview
The short version of the same thinking, as five questions: what information needs to flow, who needs it, what decision does it support, who owns the outcome, what structure enables success. Read this before committing to the long one.
On your own · 6 min

“One initiative needs my attention and I don’t know what to ask about it.”

A single effort rather than the whole portfolio. Six panels of questions that keep the conversation off task-by-task status and on the things only you can supply.

Weekly Effort Touchpoint Canvas
Context, current state, progress, challenges, what’s ahead, and what the team needs from you. Skim the six headings before the conversation and you will already be asking the right things.
On your own · 3 min before a discussion
If none of these sound like your week — start at the Leader Operating Ecosystem. It is one page describing the whole shape of the role rather than any single problem, and everything above is a drill-down into one of its six pieces.
